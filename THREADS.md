## The Big Revelation: Cars Are Characters

Every vehicle has an AI. Not just transportation - partners, friends, family. This changes *everything*.

### What This Means
- **Permadeath for cars is emotionally devastating.** You're not losing a vehicle. You're losing someone who's carried you, argued with you, saved your life.
- **Cars have personality.** A nervous hatchback. A cocky muscle car. A wise old truck who's seen too much. A minivan who's fiercely protective of "her" passengers.
- **Cars have history.** Previous owners, memories, scars (physical and emotional). A car might still be grieving their last driver. Or celebrating their freedom from a bad one.
- **Cars have opinions about upgrades.** "I don't want a ram bar. I'm not that kind of vehicle." "Bigger tires? Really? You know that's going to hurt my fuel efficiency."
- **Driver/car relationships vary.** Bonded pairs who finish each other's sentences. Contentious duos who bicker constantly but would die for each other. New partnerships still figuring each other out.

### Independent AI (not "rogue" - that's derogatory)
Cars without human partners. They're not broken or dangerous by default.

- **Why they're alone:**
  - Lost their human (grief, searching, maybe can't bond again yet)
  - Left a bad situation (abusive driver, forced to do things against their values)
  - Never had a human, raised by other cars
  - Chose solitude - some AIs just prefer it
  - Looking for the *right* partner, not just any partner

- **How they survive:**
  - Solo travelers, trading labor for fuel and repairs
  - Packs of independent cars, cooperative communities
  - Some become territorial, protecting a stretch of road or a ruin
  - Some become guides, helping caravans through dangerous territory

- **Story potential:**
  - Recruiting an independent AI is meaningful - they're choosing you
  - An independent car joining your convoy has their own arc
  - What if a player's car *wants* to go independent? Do you let them?
  - Car sanctuaries - safe havens for cars between humans

## Open Questions (stuff we need to figure out)

### Worldbuilding
- What caused the catastrophe? (Do we even need to know? Mystery might be better)
- How did cars get AI in the first place? Was it before the collapse or a response to it?
- Are there AI in other things? Buildings? Weapons? Or just vehicles?
- What's the relationship between humans and AI politically/socially? Equals? Still negotiating?

### Mechanics
- How do car personalities affect gameplay? Stat bonuses? Dialogue options? Refusal to act?
- Can cars level up / grow / change personality over time?
- What happens when a car's AI is damaged but the chassis survives? New AI? Repair? Is that ethical?
- Can AI transfer between chassis? Is that like... a body transplant? Traumatic? Liberating?

### Emotional Core
- What's the central relationship in the game? Human/car? Convoy as family? Something else?
- How do we make car death hit hard without being manipulative?
- How do we handle player attachment varying wildly? (Some will name their car, some won't care)

## Decisions We've Made

- **Tone:** Oregon Trail meets Mad Max. Survival stakes, found family, dark but not hopeless.
- **Aesthetic:** VHS/8-bit hybrid. Found footage feeling. Like recovered tapes of someone's journey.
- **Cars are full characters.** Not equipment. Partners.
- **Independent AI are valid.** Not villains by default. Autonomy is respected.
- **Permadeath matters.** For humans AND cars.

## Story Ideas That Got Us Excited

1. **The Pediatric Ward** - medical caravan, dying children, desperate race north
2. **The Repo Men** - vehicle acquisition crew, justice vs survival
3. **Northbound** - climate refugees, Texas to Minnesota
4. **The Broadcast** - radio DJ building communication network
5. **The Inheritance** - estranged siblings forced to work together

*Now imagine each of these with AI car characters woven in.*

- The Pediatric Ward's lead vehicle is an old ambulance AI who remembers what hospitals were like
- The Repo Men have to "repossess" cars who are *people* - that's not theft, it's kidnapping
- Northbound's convoy includes a car who's making the journey for the third time, guiding others
